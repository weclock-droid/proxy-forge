# Proxy Forge

Make your own Magic: The Gathering proxy cards in the browser. Pick any image, type the card text, and print.

**Use it here:** https://weclock-droid.github.io/proxy-forge/

## What it does

- **Any image as art.** Choose a file, drag it in, or paste it with Ctrl+V. Drag the card to move the art and scroll on it to zoom.
- **Card text with mana symbols.** Type `{T}`, `{W}`, `{U}`, `{B}`, `{R}`, `{G}`, `{C}`, `{X}`, `{3}` or hybrids like `{W/U}`. Text in (parentheses) turns italic like reminder text, and `*asterisks*` make anything italic.
- **Four looks:** full art with a fade, full art with a text panel, a classic frame in any color, or a **custom layout**.
- **Custom layout:** every piece of text is a layer you can drag, resize and rotate, with 20 fonts, outlines, shadows, 3D lettering, vertical text, and box or torn-strip backgrounds. Start from a ready-made design (comic cover, torn strips, poster, vertical title, handwritten) and change anything. Two color pickers (and eight color pairs) recolor every box, strip and its text at once.
- **Mana symbol styles:** classic, flat, outline, solid color, symbol only or pixel, in any color, for the whole card or one layer.
- **Your own symbols:** upload an image for any symbol code. Replace a built-in one like `{B}`, or make a new one like `{HEART}`.
- **Set symbol and rarity:** common, uncommon, rare or mythic. Upload your own set symbol and it's colored by rarity automatically, or upload a separate image for each rarity. Looking up a card fills in its set code, and the official set symbol is fetched from Scryfall and colored by rarity; type any set code (like MH3) to use that set's symbol.
- **One-click styles:** over twenty ready-made looks, including classic, borderless, comic, noir, pop art, risograph, oil painting, starship console, modern game case, VHS tape, playtest misprint, worn paperback, pulp comic and retro game box, each previewed with your own card.
- **Wrist computer layout:** a rugged olive device drawn like an illustration, with each part a crisp-outlined piece laid on the one below: segmented side rails in the card's colors, screws, a vent, glowing glass indicator bulbs, a curved 4:3 CRT screen with heavy scanlines and a green readout, a dark type bar and recessed rules plates.
- **Storybook gold:** a textured gold-foil border with ribbon banners for the name, cost and type, and a framed art window.
- **Vintage pack:** a retro packaging design with a big condensed name, a pinstriped lower half and a framed art window. Great for basic lands.
- **Comic cover:** a 1970s comic-book cover with a price box, a publisher box, a "presents" banner, a huge 3D title, the flavor text in a speech balloon and the rules in yellow caption boxes.
- **Fantasy paperback:** an old paperback cover with art tinted in the card's color (teal for blue, green for green, red-orange for red and so on), the artist's name as the author, an outlined blackletter title with a 3D edge, dark fades behind the text, and a price strip down the spine with the mana cost.
- **Scales & scroll:** the name on a smoky, flame-edged plume of dragon breath that turns down into the dragon's neck; its scaled body coils down the right side while another loop comes in from off the left edge and back out. The type line sits on an ornate pointed plaque over a parchment rules panel.
- **Pixel hero:** pixel-art art (a new filter) in a 4:3 window, pixel mana symbols, and chunky game-style text boxes in a pixel font.
- **Playtest cards:** a plain cream card with black serif text, the mana cost written as letters (`2W`, `TAP`), and photocopied art in a pasted-in window, like a homemade playtest card.
- **Print errors:** toner specks like a photocopy, a color misprint (some text and art printed in magenta or cyan, a yellow ghost of the card, color-bar stripes) or a bad print run with a miscut. Works with any style.
- **Art window:** in the custom layout, an art window layer puts the picture in a box you can move and resize. Drag inside it to move the picture, or drag its edge to move the window.
- **Neon street:** full-bleed art under a thin glowing frame with cut corners in the card's color, a cut-corner cost badge, a big condensed name low on the card, the artist credit running up the right edge and a dark fade behind the rules.
- **Fan-favorite homemade looks:**
  - **Desktop dialog:** a 90s computer window. Basic lands ask "Tap for 1 mana?" with OK and Cancel buttons.
  - **RPG item screen:** a game inventory panel with MANA and MANA VALUE stats.
  - **Board game card:** "PAY 1", a big name and simple art on a pastel card.
  - **Fantasy album:** BASIC LAND in big shadowed letters, a giant mana symbol and pastel paper in the land's color.
  - **Vintage sports card:** a rounded photo, a yellow name panel and worn edges.
  - **Altered (paint pen):** pink blackletter and white marker lettering over the art.
  - **Linocut print:** carved color blocks, linocut art in the card's color and a sunburst mana symbol.
  - **Hand-drawn:** pencil lettering on paper with ruled boxes.
  - **Blood drip:** stencil-cut black and white art, a red paint-drip strip down the side and a red blackletter name.
  - **Pixel glow:** a dark card with the art turned into glowing pixels in the card's color, a thin colored frame and a pixel-font name.
  - **Neon line art:** the art reduced to bright colored outlines on black, with chunky handwritten lettering.
  - **Retro poster:** a groovy 70s title over a rounded window, a big mana circle and pastel paper in the card's color.
  - **X-ray:** the art glowing like film on a light box, a thin tall name and handwritten rules.
- **Creature collector:** full art inside a thick rounded border in the card's color, a light header with a small type tag, a bold name and the cost, rules on a soft light fade, and power/toughness in a silver pill.
- **Monster card:** a silver late-90s monster card with the name at the top, a row of level stars (one per point of mana value), the art in a beveled picture frame and big ATK / DEF numbers from power and toughness. There's also a black-and-white manga version.
- **Kanji landscape:** lands with the art filling the whole card and the land's name in Japanese (平地, 島, 沼, 山, 森) on a tall rounded plaque and its mana symbol below, colored to match. Pair it with **Make basic lands…** for a whole set.
- **Meme styles:** an image macro with huge outlined top and bottom text, a demotivational poster, and a deep-fried version with a crunchy, oversaturated art filter.
- **Keyword highlighting:** in a custom layout, a text layer can show keywords like Flying, Haste, First strike or Scry as colored capitals or as highlighted tags. Neon street tags them in the card's color.
- **More styles:** stained glass (the art becomes glass panes with lead lines under an arched window), tarot card, illuminated manuscript (with a decorated first letter), woodblock print, blueprint, neon synthwave, pencil sketch, atlas map, Polaroid and a token frame.
- **Styles that follow the card's color:** Vintage pack, Storybook gold, Scales & scroll, Tarot, Illuminated and Woodblock print (and Neon street) take the card's color, so an Island and a Mountain made in the same style look different. The color comes from the mana cost, like real frames: one color, gold for two or more, silver for artifacts, gray for colorless, and lands by the mana they make.
- **Style groups:** the one-click styles are sorted into Classic, Fantasy, Retro & games, Comics & pulp, Sci-fi and Print & paper.
- **Your own styles:** make a card look the way you like, give it a name and save it. It appears under My styles with a live preview, and keeps the frame, fonts, filters, effects and every custom layer, but not the card text or art. Export your styles to a file to back them up or share them, and import files from others.
- **Random art from your own pictures:** point Proxy Forge at a folder on your computer (or use your art library) and Remix's **Art** button, deck lists and the basic land maker pick random pictures from it, each one once before any repeats. In Chrome and Edge the folder is remembered between visits; in other browsers it's read once per visit. Your pictures never leave your computer.
- **Your art library:** every image you add is kept in your browser, with a thumbnail, so you can reuse it on other cards. Save searched art there too, and remove anything you don't want.
- **Starship console layout:** a retro-futuristic computer-panel frame with pastel elbow bars and orange capitals, colored to match the card. Lands get a tall art window with their mana colors in the corner.
- **Saga cards:** chapter lines like `I — …` or `II, III — …` get numbered chapter badges and dividers in every layout.
- **Art filters:** black & white, sepia, duotone, duotone in the card's color, halftone dots, color halftone (pop art), posterize, film grain, oil painting, photocopy, pixel art, stained glass, woodcut, linocut in the card's color, stencil, neon outlines, glowing pixels, x-ray, blueprint and pencil sketch, with color pairs to pick from. Your own pictures start with no filter, even in styles that use one, so you choose whether to add one.
- **Proxy or playtest:** the two words mean the same thing here. A **Card wording** setting puts either one on every label (like PLAYTEST ✦ 16-BIT ✦ CARD SYSTEM) and on the bottom note, or keeps each style's own wording.
- **Wear & aging:** four levels, from lightly used to falling apart, so a card looks like a well-read paperback or an old comic: yellowed paper, rubbed-off ink, a cracked spine, creases, chipped edges, frayed corners, tears, a folded corner, crinkled laminate and old price-sticker residue. Shuffle damage for a different pattern.
- **Stickers:** put any text on a paper sticker, a barcode sticker or a crossed-out "sale price" sticker.
- **42 free fonts** from Google Fonts, grouped into classic, fantasy, horror, display, comic, sci-fi & retro, and handwritten.
- **Remix for ideas:** one click gives you a random real card, public-domain art picked to match it (from Wikimedia Commons, credited on the page), and a random look. Roll just the text, the art or the look, and lock the parts you want to keep. The first time you open the page, you get a fresh remix. Art is chosen from the card itself: its creature types first (a Vampire searches for vampires), then a word from its name, then a mood for its color. Lands get landscapes instead, either paintings or photos.
- **Your card:** enter your birthday and pick a mood (calm, balanced, hopeful, passionate, brooding, dreamy, or let the stars decide) to get "your card". Your star sign leans it toward its element: Pisces gets sea creatures and sea art, Leo gets cats and nobles, Scorpio gets scorpions and insects, and "let the stars decide" uses your sign's color. The same birthday and mood always give the same card, and with **A new card each day** ticked you get a fresh one every day. Your birthday stays in your browser.
- **Search for art:** type what you want (like "ship in a storm") and pick a source: [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org) (museum paintings from the Google Art Project), the [Wellcome Collection](https://wellcomecollection.org) (old prints and drawings of medicine, alchemy and monsters) the [Rijksmuseum](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl) (Dutch paintings and prints), **Wikimedia Commons photos** (CC0 and public-domain photographs) or the [NASA Image Library](https://images.nasa.gov) (space, Earth and aircraft). Only public-domain or CC0 images are shown, and the art is credited on the page with its title, artist, license, source and the words you searched.
- **Look up real cards:** type a card name and the name, cost, type, rules, flavor, power/toughness, rarity and collector line are filled in from [Scryfall](https://scryfall.com). Pick a printing or the back side, and optionally use the card's official art.
- **Deck lists:** paste a list like `4 Lightning Bolt` (Arena and MTGO exports work too) and every card is added to the print sheet with its number of copies, in the look of the card you're editing. Double-faced cards get their back face too. Each card gets public-domain art picked to match it, its official art, or plain placeholder art.
- **Basic lands:** pick which basics (and Wastes or snow-covered versions), how many of each, and whether every copy gets different art: public-domain landscapes, official basic land art or placeholders. They're added to the print sheet in your current style.
- **Print sheets:** add cards (or card images you already have), set copies, and save a PDF with 9 cards per page at real card size (2.5 × 3.5″), with cut marks. Print at 100% / "Actual size".
- **Single cards:** save a PNG at 600, 800 or 1200 dpi. **Print-shop PNG** saves at 800 dpi with ⅛″ bleed and square corners (2200 × 3000 px), which is what online print shops ask for.
- **Whole sheet for a print shop:** **Save all as print-shop PNGs (zip)** saves every card on the print sheet as its own 800 dpi bleed PNG, named after the card, in one zip. Pick a **card back** (a plain playtest back or your own image) and it's added as `back.png`. Finished card images that already have a ⅛″ bleed edge are detected: the bleed is trimmed for home-printed PDFs and kept for print-shop zips.
- **Add bleed to images you already have:** **Add bleed to images…** takes card images saved without bleed and adds a ⅛″ edge by mirroring each card's outer edges outward, saving 800 dpi PNGs (one file, or a zip for several). Images that already have bleed are skipped.
- **Artist credit:** you're reminded before saving a card with no artist credit, and an **AI-generated** tick box adds the model name to the credit.
- **Sharpness meter** also shows the art sizes print-shop guides recommend (1200 × 900 for framed art, 1500 × 2000 for full-card art) and suggests upscaling smaller pictures.

Everything runs in your browser. Nothing is uploaded anywhere, and your print sheet is saved in your own browser. Card lookups and deck lists send only the card names to Scryfall, Remix asks Scryfall for a random card and Wikimedia Commons for art, and art search sends only the words you type to the source you picked.

## Run it offline

Download `proxy-forge.html` and open it in your browser. It needs an internet connection for the fonts and for building PDFs.
